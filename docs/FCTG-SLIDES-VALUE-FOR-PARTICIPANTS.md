## FROM VIBE TO AGENTIC (Ch 5)

## Slide 21 — From vibe to agentic (intro)  
**Value:** Introduces the core distinction (creative flow, intuition, conversational, rapid experimentation) before the practical split.

## Slide 22 — Exploration vs execution  
**Value:** Clarifies when to use “vibe” (open, exploratory) vs “agentic” (directed) and the three readiness signals (outcome, constraints, success), so they know when to switch modes.

## Slide 23 — Prompts vs briefs  
**Value:** Shows side-by-side examples (UI, copy, research) so they see the difference between a prompt and a brief and why clarity of direction matters.

## Slide 24 — Anatomy of a brief  
**Value:** Gives a reusable structure (outcome, context, constraints, deliverable, success checks) and an example so they can write delegatable briefs immediately.

## Slide 25 — From outputs to direction  
**Value:** Reframes the design role (less isolated outputs, more defining outcomes, shaping context, reviewing and steering) so they see how their job evolves, not vanishes.

## Slide 26 — Context carries forward  
**Value:** Explains why externalizing context (notes, decisions, patterns) improves agentic work across sessions, so they invest in shared context.

## Slide 27 — Staying in control  
**Value:** Names guardrails (scope, file rules, budgets, cleanup) and failure modes to watch for, so they feel in control and know what to correct.

## Slide 28 — Wipeout 2097 mental model  
**Value:** Crystallizes “you steer, AI is the speed boost” in one memorable image so the balance of control and acceleration sticks.

## DESIGN PRACTICE (Ch 6)

## Slide 29 — Design practice (intro)  
**Value:** Opens the “how this shows up in practice” block (research, synthesis, prototyping, validation) so they expect concrete workflows.

## Slide 30 — Execution compresses  
**Value:** Makes the then/now shift visible (execution-heavy → more judgment, direction, decisions) so they see why their role is shifting and where to lean in.

## Slide 31 — Research to synthesis  
**Value:** Shows how agentic synthesis turns messy inputs into themes, opportunities, and direction, with them reviewing and deciding — so they see a real path from research to action.

## Slide 32 — Agentic prototyping  
**Value:** Shows how to ground an agent in a design system and brief to get parallel variants and live previews, so they can try agentic prototyping with existing assets.

## Slide 33 — API-backed prototypes

## Slide 34 — Testing and validation  
**Value:** Shows how AI can support test plans, scripts, synthesis, and next steps while they run sessions and judge findings, so validation stays human-led but faster.

## Slide 35 — From Figma to handoff  
**Value:** Shows how a flow + context + clear ask becomes implementation-ready guidance (summary, rules, open questions), so handoff is clearer and context carries forward.

## Slide 36 — What changes for teams  
**Value:** Summarises how roles shift from production to direction so teams can have an honest conversation about who does what with AI.

## AI-READY SYSTEMS (Ch 7)

## Slide 37 — AI-ready systems (intro)  
**Value:** Frames the next section as “systems, context, and feedback that make AI useful in real work,” not just prompts.

## Slide 38 — AI does not fix a messy workflow  
**Value:** Sets the expectation that AI exposes gaps (source of truth, goal clarity, review speed) so they improve the workflow instead of hoping AI will hide problems.

## Slide 39 — Where good context comes from  
**Value:** Lists concrete inputs (Figma, research, product rules, live product) and the idea “pick what changes the answer” so they know what to feed and what to leave out.

## Slide 40 — What good output looks like  
**Value:** Defines good output as clear, reviewable, and actionable (clearer flows, missing states, guidance, next steps) so they know what to ask for and how to judge it.

## Slide 41 — Design systems as shared context  
**Value:** Positions the design system as inspectable context that leads to on-brand output, so they see design systems as an AI enabler.

## Slide 42 — Automated testing as guardrails  
**Value:** Explains how tests protect what works, validate real behaviour, and support confident shipping when AI helps make changes.

## Slide 43 — When to use more structure  
**Value:** Gives a simple rule: more structure when flows are complex, shipping risk is higher, or tasks are repeatable — so they don’t over- or under-structure.
