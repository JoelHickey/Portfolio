## One-line transitions (say these if you need a bridge)

- **After Slide 1 (agenda):** “We’ll start with why design still holds, then how AI is changing the moments that matter.”
- **Into Slide 16 (Mechanisms):** “So far we’ve looked at the why and the feel. Now: how it actually works — models, agents, and what ‘agentic’ means.”
- **Into Slide 21 (From vibe to agentic):** “That’s the machinery. The next question is: how do you work with it? That’s the shift from vibe to agentic.”
- **Into Slide 29 (Design practice):** “Here’s what that looks like in your real practice: research, prototyping, validation, handoff.”
- **Into Slide 37 (AI-ready systems):** “For this to work in your systems, you need the right context and guardrails. That’s this section.”
- **Into Slide 44 (Activity):** “You’ve seen the map. Now you drive. We’ll do a short activity: one real flow, AI to explore and refine, then share back.”
- **Into Slide 46 (Close):** “We’re into the close. I’ll show how this deck was built, then mindset and takeaways.”

## Activity — what to say and when

### Before they start (Slide 45)

- “You have about 60 minutes total. Work on your own flow; we’ll share in small groups at the end.”
- “Use Miro to capture your start (goal, pain, context), then Explore and Refine. Figma Make is there if you want to generate or refine UI. You don’t have to use both — pick what fits your flow.”
- “Your outputs: one improved flow, one gap or edge case you spotted, and one risk you’d validate next. That’s what you’ll share.”

### At 15 min

- “If you’re still in Start, that’s fine. If you’re in Explore, you should be seeing a few different directions from AI. Pick one to take into Refine.”

### At 35 min

- “Most of you should be in Refine: one chosen direction, mapping the improved flow. Start thinking about the one gap and one risk you’ll share.”

### At 50 min

- “Five minutes to wrap. Finish your flow and your one gap + one risk. We’ll share in groups of three or four in a moment.”

### When they share (groups of 3–4)

- “In your group: each person gets a minute — the flow you improved, one gap or edge case, one risk to validate. No need to present to the whole room unless we have time.”

## If something goes wrong

- **Deck won’t load:** You have the structure in the value doc; you can talk through sections and use Miro/Figma only for the activity.
- **Miro access issues:** They can use a single board together, or paper + photo to Miro later. The goal is “one flow, one gap, one risk” — the tool is secondary.
- **“We’re behind”:** Drop or shorten: Slide 14 (Mystical Code), Slide 19 (Multi-agent), or 41–43 (design systems / testing / structure). Keep 22–24, 28, 30, 31–32, 38–40, 44–45, 48–49.
- **Tough question:** “I don’t have a crisp answer — I’ll note it and we can follow up” is fine. You have Q&A prep below for the common ones.

## Q&A — short answers you can use

**“Will AI replace designers?”**  
“The deck’s argument is no: execution compresses, direction and judgment matter more. You’re steering; AI is the speed boost. Mindset slide sums it up — keep judgment human.”

**“When do I use vibe vs agentic?”**  
“When you’re still figuring out the problem or direction, use vibe — open prompts, fast iteration. When you can name the outcome, constraints, and what good looks like, switch to agentic and write a brief.”

**“What if our workflow is messy?”**  
“Slide 38: AI doesn’t fix a messy workflow, it makes the gaps visible. Use that to improve source of truth, goal clarity, and review speed — then AI gets more useful.”

**“What’s the one thing to try first?”**  
“Pick one real flow. Write a short brief: outcome, context, constraints, deliverable, success. Give it to an agent or tool and review the output. That’s the loop — then iterate.”

**“How do we get design systems AI-ready?”**  
“Treat them as shared context: tokens, components, docs the agent can see. You don’t need a new system — make what you have inspectable and referenced in the brief.”
